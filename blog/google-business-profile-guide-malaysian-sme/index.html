Google Business Profile: a simple setup guide for Malaysian SMEs.

By Ken Wong · 23 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

If you run a clinic, a renovation firm, a café or a supply business in Malaysia, there is a good chance your Google Business Profile is the first thing a new customer sees. Not your website. Not your Facebook page. The little box on the map with your name, stars and phone button.

The good news is that it is free, and you control it. The bad news is that most profiles are set up once and then left alone. This guide walks you through what to fix, in order.

Why your Google Business Profile matters.

When someone searches for something local, Google often shows a map and three or four businesses first. That area is built from Google Business Profiles.

Take a dental clinic in Penang as an example. Customers might search for:

  • "dental clinic Bayan Lepas"
  • "klinik gigi dekat saya"
  • "牙科 槟城"

Three languages, three different searches. In each case, the customer sees a handful of profiles and picks one. If yours is half empty, you lose that click to someone who finished theirs.

Step 1: Claim it and check the basics.

Search for your business name on Google. If a profile appears, claim it. If not, create one at business.google.com.

Then check the boring details. They matter most:

  • Business name exactly as it appears on your signboard
  • Address and map pin in the right place
  • Phone number that someone actually answers
  • Opening hours, including public holidays and festive periods
  • Website link

Wrong hours are the fastest way to earn a bad review. Update them before Hari Raya, Chinese New Year and Deepavali, not after.

Step 2: Choose the right categories.

Your primary category tells Google what you are. It is one of the strongest signals on the profile.

Be specific. "Dentist" is better than "Health". "Kitchen remodeler" is better than "Contractor" if kitchens are what you do. Then add secondary categories for other services you genuinely offer.

A quick test: look at the profiles of the businesses that rank near you. Which categories do they use? You can see this with a free browser extension or by checking how they describe themselves.

Step 3: Write for the languages your customers use.

This is where many Malaysian profiles fall short. Your customers do not all search in English.

Write your business description and service list in plain, natural language. If your customers search in Malay or Chinese, include those words where they fit. Do not stuff keywords. Write it the way you would explain your business to a new customer at the counter.

Step 4: Add photos, services and posts.

Profiles with real photos look alive. Add:

  • The front of your shop, so people can find you
  • Your team, at work
  • Your products, dishes or finished projects
  • The inside, including parking or access if it helps

Use your own photos. Stock images do not build trust.

Then fill in the Services or Products section with short descriptions. Add a post when you have an offer-free update, such as new opening hours or a new service. Keep it simple.

Step 5: Ask for reviews and reply to all of them.

Reviews are how strangers decide whether to trust you.

Ask happy customers at the right moment, for example just after a successful treatment or a finished job. Send them your review link by WhatsApp. Never pay for reviews or write your own. Google can remove them, and customers can usually tell.

Reply to every review:

  • For good reviews, say thank you and mention something specific.
  • For bad reviews, stay calm, apologise where it is fair, and offer to fix it offline.

Other customers read your replies as much as the reviews.

Step 6: Think about AI search too.

People now ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Google AI Overviews questions such as "which clinic near me does teeth whitening?" These tools need clear, consistent facts about your business. A complete profile, matching details on your website and honest reviews all help.

Nobody outside those companies knows exactly how they choose what to show. So we do not promise a result. What you can do is make your information accurate and easy to read. Our page on AI search visibility explains how this works in more detail.

What nobody can promise.

If someone guarantees you the top spot on Google Maps, be careful. Rankings depend on many things, including how close the customer is to you, how relevant your profile is, and how well known your business appears. Nobody can control all of that.

What you can do is remove the easy mistakes. That alone puts you ahead of many competitors.

A 20-minute checklist.

Open your profile now and tick these off:

  1. Name, address and phone are correct
  2. Opening hours are up to date
  3. Primary category is the most specific one
  4. At least five of your own photos are uploaded
  5. Services are listed with short descriptions
  6. Your last five reviews have replies

FAQ.

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile costs nothing. You only pay if you choose to run adverts separately.

How long until I see a difference?

It varies. Some changes, like correcting your hours, show quickly. Rankings can take weeks or longer and depend on your competition. Nobody can give you an exact date.

Do I still need a website if I have a profile?

Yes. Your profile gets people to find you, and your website gives them the detail to choose you. They work best together.
